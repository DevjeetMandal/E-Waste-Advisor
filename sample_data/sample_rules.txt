[Category: IT and Telecommunication Equipment]
This category covers items such as computers, laptops, printers, mobile
phones, tablets, and networking equipment like routers and modems. These
devices typically contain circuit boards with recoverable copper, gold,
silver, and palladium, alongside components such as lithium-ion batteries
and LCD/LED screens that require careful handling. Improper dismantling of
circuit boards can release lead and other heavy metals. These items should
be handed to an authorized collection point, producer take-back program, or
a registered dismantler/recycler rather than an informal scrap dealer.

[Category: Consumer Electronics]
This category includes televisions, refrigerators, washing machines, air
conditioners, and microwave ovens. Refrigerators and air conditioners may
contain refrigerant gases and insulation foams that require specialised
handling to avoid environmental release. CRT and older television screens
contain leaded glass. Large consumer electronics should be returned through
manufacturer take-back schemes or given to authorized recyclers equipped to
handle refrigerant recovery and glass separation.

[Category: Batteries and Power Storage]
This category includes lithium-ion batteries, power banks, and battery
packs from phones and laptops. Damaged lithium-ion batteries can pose fire
risks if punctured, crushed, or exposed to heat, and should never be placed
in regular household trash. They should be taken to a designated battery
collection point or an authorized e-waste collection center for safe
handling.

[Category: Lighting Equipment]
This category includes CFL and fluorescent tube lights, which may contain
small amounts of mercury. These should not be broken or disposed of with
general waste; they should be returned to a collection point equipped to
handle mercury-containing waste.

[General Guidance: Authorized Channels vs Informal Recycling]
Selling old electronics to informal, unregistered scrap dealers often
results in manual dismantling without protective equipment, which can
expose workers to lead, mercury, cadmium, and other toxic substances, and
typically results in valuable recoverable materials being lost or
improperly processed. Using an authorized collection center, manufacturer
take-back program, or a registered dismantler/recycler is the safer and
more resource-efficient option, and is the approach reflected in India's
formal e-waste management framework.
